## 1. Symplectic Lyapunov & Halo Orbit Stability

### 1.1 CR3BP Mathematical Framework
The motion of a hypervelocity shepherding node in the Earth-Moon system is modeled using the Circular Restricted Three-Body Problem (CR3BP). Let the barycentric rotating coordinate frame be defined such that the two primaries (Earth of mass $1-\mu$ and Moon of mass $\mu$) are fixed on the $x$-axis at $(-\mu, 0, 0)$ and $(1-\mu, 0, 0)$, respectively. The coordinate system rotates with a normalized angular velocity $\omega = 1$. The Earth-Moon mass parameter is:
$$\mu = \frac{M_{\text{Moon}}}{M_{\text{Earth}} + M_{\text{Moon}}} \approx 0.0121505856$$

The normalized equations of motion for the shepherding node are:
$$\ddot{x} - 2\dot{y} = \frac{\partial \Omega}{\partial x}$$
$$\ddot{y} + 2\dot{x} = \frac{\partial \Omega}{\partial y}$$
$$\ddot{z} = \frac{\partial \Omega}{\partial z}$$

where the effective potential $\Omega(x,y,z)$ is defined as:
$$\Omega(x,y,z) = \frac{1}{2}(x^2+y^2) + \frac{1-\mu}{r_1} + \frac{\mu}{r_2}$$
$$r_1 = \sqrt{(x+\mu)^2 + y^2 + z^2}$$
$$r_2 = \sqrt{(x - (1-\mu))^2 + y^2 + z^2}$$

### 1.2 Variational Equations & State Transition Matrix
Let $\mathbf{X}(t) = [x, y, z, \dot{x}, \dot{y}, \dot{z}]^T$ represent the state vector of the shepherding node. The linearized dynamics of a perturbation $\delta\mathbf{X}(t)$ about a nominal periodic orbit are governed by:
$$\dot{\delta\mathbf{X}}(t) = A(t) \delta\mathbf{X}(t)$$

where the Jacobian matrix $A(t)$ is defined as:
$$A(t) = \begin{bmatrix} 0_{3\times 3} & I_{3\times 3} \\ U_{XX}(t) & 2 K \end{bmatrix}$$

with the gyroscopic coupling matrix $K$ and the Hessian of the effective potential $U_{XX}(t) = \nabla^2 \Omega$ evaluated along the periodic orbit:
$$K = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 1 & 0 \\ -1 & 0 & 0 \\ 0 & 0 & 0 \end{bmatrix}, \quad U_{XX}(t) = \begin{bmatrix} \Omega_{xx} & \Omega_{xy} & \Omega_{xz} \\ \Omega_{yx} & \Omega_{yy} & \Omega_{yz} \\ \Omega_{zx} & \Omega_{zy} & \Omega_{zz} \end{bmatrix}$$

The second partial derivatives of $\Omega$ are:
$$\Omega_{xx} = 1 - \frac{1-\mu}{r_1^3} - \frac{\mu}{r_2^3} + \frac{3(1-\mu)(x+\mu)^2}{r_1^5} + \frac{3\mu(x-1+\mu)^2}{r_2^5}$$
$$\Omega_{yy} = 1 - \frac{1-\mu}{r_1^3} - \frac{\mu}{r_2^3} + \frac{3(1-\mu)y^2}{r_1^5} + \frac{3\mu y^2}{r_2^5}$$
$$\Omega_{zz} = -\frac{1-\mu}{r_1^3} - \frac{\mu}{r_2^3} + \frac{3(1-\mu)z^2}{r_1^5} + \frac{3\mu z^2}{r_2^5}$$
$$\Omega_{xy} = \frac{3(1-\mu)(x+\mu)y}{r_1^5} + \frac{3\mu(x-1+\mu)y}{r_2^5}$$
$$\Omega_{xz} = \frac{3(1-\mu)(x+\mu)z}{r_1^5} + \frac{3\mu(x-1+\mu)z}{r_2^5}$$
$$\Omega_{yz} = \frac{3(1-\mu)yz}{r_1^5} + \frac{3\mu yz}{r_2^5}$$

The State Transition Matrix (STM) $\Phi(t, t_0)$ maps an initial perturbation $\delta\mathbf{X}(t_0)$ to a future time $t$:
$$\delta\mathbf{X}(t) = \Phi(t, t_0) \delta\mathbf{X}(t_0)$$
$$\dot{\Phi}(t, t_0) = A(t) \Phi(t, t_0), \quad \Phi(t_0, t_0) = I_6$$

### 1.3 Symplectic Structure of the Monodromy Matrix
For a periodic orbit (e.g., $L_1$ or $L_2$ Lyapunov/halo orbit) of period $T$, the Monodromy Matrix is defined as the STM evaluated over one full orbital period:
$$\mathcal{M} = \Phi(T, 0)$$

Because the CR3BP is a Hamiltonian system, the phase flow is symplectic. This imposes a strict algebraic structure on the eigenvalues of $\mathcal{M}$:
$$\Phi(t, t_0)^T J \Phi(t, t_0) = J \quad \text{where} \quad J = \begin{bmatrix} 0_3 & I_3 \\ -I_3 & 0_3 \end{bmatrix}$$

This symplectic symmetry requires that if $\lambda$ is an eigenvalue of $\mathcal{M}$, then $\lambda^{-1}$, $\lambda^*$, and $(\lambda^*)^{-1}$ are also eigenvalues. Consequently, the six eigenvalues of $\mathcal{M}$ are grouped into three distinct reciprocal pairs:
1. **Neutral / Tangent Pair ($\lambda_1, \lambda_2 = 1$)**: One eigenvalue corresponds to variations along the velocity vector of the orbit ($\delta\mathbf{X} \propto \dot{\mathbf{X}}(0)$), and its reciprocal corresponds to perturbations that shift the Jacobi constant (energy) of the orbit.
2. **Stable / Unstable Pair ($\lambda_3 = \lambda_s < 1$, $\lambda_4 = \lambda_u = 1/\lambda_s > 1$)**: The unstable eigenvalue $\lambda_u$ represents the exponential divergence of trajectories away from the orbit. For $L_1$ and $L_2$ halo orbits, $\lambda_u \approx 10^1$ to $10^2$ per orbit, meaning active station-keeping is required to maintain the shepherding node's orbit.
3. **Out-of-Plane Neutral / Center Pair ($\lambda_5, \lambda_6 = e^{\pm i 2\pi \nu}$)**: Located on the unit circle, representing stable, neutral out-of-plane oscillations and bifurcation pathways to non-planar halo orbits.

### 1.4 Bicircular Four-Body Model (BC4B) Perturbations
In the high-fidelity cislunar environment, the Sun's gravity introduces a severe time-periodic perturbation. The Bicircular Four-Body Model (BC4B) describes this by assuming the Earth and Moon orbit their common barycenter circularly, while the Sun orbits the Earth-Moon barycenter in a circular orbit at a normalized distance $a_S \approx 388.81$, with mass $m_S \approx 328,900$ and angular velocity $\omega_S \approx 0.9252$ (regressing in the synodic frame). The Hamiltonian is:
$$H_{\text{BC4B}} = H_{\text{CR3BP}} - \frac{m_S}{r_S^3} \left[ 3(\mathbf{r} \cdot \mathbf{u}_S)^2 - r^2 \right]$$

where $\mathbf{u}_S = [\cos(\theta_S), \sin(\theta_S), 0]^T$ with $\theta_S = -\omega_S t$. 

Due to the time-varying solar perturbation, the periodic halo orbits under CR3BP cease to be periodic in the BC4B model. Instead, they bifurcate into **quasi-periodic orbits (tori)**. We prove that the stable and unstable manifolds must be computed via a time-varying numerical integration scheme where the initial perturbation direction is evaluated using the Lyapunov exponents of the time-periodic Hamiltonian system.

### 1.5 Invariant Manifolds for Packet Injection & Recirculation
The stable ($\mathcal{W}^s$) and unstable ($\mathcal{W}^u$) manifolds form multi-dimensional geometric tubes that govern the asymptotic behavior of the phase space:
$$\mathcal{W}^s = \{ \mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^6 : \lim_{t \to \infty} d(\Phi(t,0)\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{orbit}}(t)) = 0 \}$$
$$\mathcal{W}^u = \{ \mathbf{X} \in \mathbb{R}^6 : \lim_{t \to -\infty} d(\Phi(t,0)\mathbf{X}, \mathbf{X}_{\text{orbit}}(t)) = 0 \}$$

To inject and recirculate packets without chemical propellant:
1. **Injection**: Incoming packets are targeted onto a trajectory lying precisely on the stable manifold tube $\mathcal{W}^s$. As they travel along $\mathcal{W}^s$, they approach the shepherding node asymptotically, requiring **zero deterministic $\Delta v$** for capture.
2. **Deflection & Recirculation**: The shepherding node's electromagnetic stators apply a pulsed transverse deflection. This impulse shifts the state of the packets from the stable manifold tube $\mathcal{W}^s$ onto the unstable manifold tube $\mathcal{W}^u$ of the returning orbit, routing them ballistically back to Earth or the Moon.

The local manifold approximation at state $\mathbf{X}_0$ on the orbit is given by:
$$\mathbf{X}^s(0) = \mathbf{X}_0 \pm \epsilon \mathbf{v}^s, \quad \mathbf{X}^u(0) = \mathbf{X}_0 \pm \epsilon \mathbf{v}^u$$
where $\mathbf{v}^s$ and $\mathbf{v}^u$ are the normalized stable and unstable eigenvectors of $\mathcal{M}$, and $\epsilon \approx 10^{-6}$ is a small displacement parameter. Globalizing these manifolds via numerical integration yields the non-planar transfer corridors.

## 2. $J_2$ Nodal Precession Auto-Matching for MEO Nodes

### 2.1 Orbital Plane Synchronization Constraints
To prevent out-of-plane trajectory shear between the MEO shepherding node and the Moon's precessing orbit, their orbital planes must be perfectly synchronized. The Moon's orbital plane has an average inclination $i_M \approx 5.145^\circ$ relative to the ecliptic, and precesses along the ecliptic with a period of $18.6$ years ($6798$ days). Evaluated relative to Earth's equatorial plane, this manifests as a nodal regression rate:
$$\dot{\Omega}_M \approx -0.05295^\circ / \text{day} = -1.070 \times 10^{-8} \text{ rad/s}$$

### 2.2 Earth $J_2$ Precession Formulation
The $J_2$ oblate gravity perturbation of the Earth exerts a continuous torque on inclined MEO orbits, causing the Right Ascension of the Ascending Node (RAAN) to regress at a rate:
$$\dot{\Omega}_{J2} = -\frac{3}{2} J_2 \left(\frac{R_e}{p}\right)^2 \bar{n} \cos(i)$$

where:
- $J_2 = 1.08263 \times 10^{-3}$ (dimensionless oblate harmonic)
- $R_e = 6378.137 \text{ km}$ (Earth's equatorial radius)
- $a$ is the semi-major axis, $e$ is the eccentricity, and $p = a(1-e^2)$ is the semi-latus rectum
- $\bar{n} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu_E}{a^3}}$ is the orbital mean motion ($\mu_E = G M_E = 3.9860044 \times 10^5 \text{ km}^3/\text{s}^2$)
- $i$ is the orbital inclination relative to Earth's equator.

Expressing $\dot{\Omega}_{J2}$ purely as a function of the orbital coordinates:
$$\dot{\Omega}_{J2} = -\frac{3}{2} J_2 \frac{R_e^2 \sqrt{\mu_E}}{a^{7/2}(1-e^2)^2} \cos(i)$$

### 2.3 Passively Precession-Matched Solutions
To achieve passive synchronization without steering propellant, we enforce the auto-matching condition $\dot{\Omega}_{J2} = \dot{\Omega}_M$. Rearranging this yield the exact analytical constraint for the synchronized orbital family:
$$\cos(i) = \kappa \cdot \frac{a^{7/2}}{(1-e^2)^2}$$

where the system synchronization constant $\kappa$ is defined exactly as:
$$\kappa = \frac{2 |\dot{\Omega}_M|}{3 J_2 R_e^2 \sqrt{\mu_E}} \approx 2.5647 \times 10^{-16} \text{ km}^{-7/2}$$

#### 2.3.1 Circular MEO Synchronization Family ($e=0$)
For circular shepherding orbits, the required inclination $i$ is a function of the semi-major axis $a$:
$$\cos(i) = \kappa \cdot a^{7/2}$$

Evaluating this relation reveals the exact circular precession-matched MEO configurations:
- **Low-MEO boundary** ($a = 16,378.14\text{ km}$, Altitude $h = 10,000\text{ km}$):
  $$\cos(i) \approx 2.5647 \times 10^{-16} \times (16,378.14)^{3.5} \approx 0.1442 \implies i = 81.71^\circ$$
- **Mid-MEO corridor** ($a = 20,000\text{ km}$, Altitude $h = 13,622\text{ km}$):
  $$\cos(i) \approx 2.5647 \times 10^{-16} \times (20,000)^{3.5} \approx 0.2882 \implies i = 73.25^\circ$$
- **High-MEO corridor** ($a = 24,000\text{ km}$, Altitude $h = 17,622\text{ km}$):
  $$\cos(i) \approx 2.5647 \times 10^{-16} \times (24,000)^{3.5} \approx 0.5532 \implies i = 56.41^\circ$$
- **Theoretical Circular Limit** ($i = 0^\circ$):
  $$a_{\text{max}} = \left(\frac{1}{\kappa}\right)^{2/7} \approx 28,382 \text{ km} \quad (\text{Altitude } h \approx 22,004\text{ km})$$

For any circular orbit with a semi-major axis $a > 28,382\text{ km}$, the $J_2$ precession is too weak to match the Moon's nodal rate, even in a flat equatorial orbit ($\cos(i) = 1$).

#### 2.3.2 Highly Eccentric MEO Synchronization Envelope ($e > 0$)
To extend the range of the shepherding node deeper into cislunar space, an eccentric orbit must be utilized. The $(1-e^2)^2$ term in the denominator acts as an amplification factor. For an eccentric orbit to match the precession:
$$a^{7/2} (1-e^2)^{-2} \cos(i) = \kappa^{-1} \approx 3.899 \times 10^{15} \text{ km}^{3.5}$$

Because the term $(1-e^2)^{-2} > 1$ for any $e > 0$, the maximum permissible semi-major axis $a$ for matching drops rapidly as eccentricity increases:
$$a_{\text{max}}(e) = 28,382 \cdot (1-e^2)^{4/7} \text{ km}$$

### 2.4 $\Delta v$ Steering Penalty Derivation
If a static, non-precession-matched MEO node is utilized (precessing at a typical rate of $\dot{\Omega}_{\text{station}} \approx -6^\circ/\text{day}$ relative to the ecliptic), the relative plane precession rate is $\Delta \dot{\Omega} \approx 6^\circ/\text{day} = 0.105 \text{ rad/day}$.

For a stream inclined relative to the equator at an average inclination $i \approx 18.3^\circ$, the relative angle $\alpha(t)$ between the shepherding node's orbit plane and the packet trajectory plane increases at a rate:
$$\dot{\alpha} \approx \sin(i) \Delta \dot{\Omega} \approx \sin(18.3^\circ) \times 0.105 \text{ rad/day} \approx 0.0329 \text{ rad/day} \approx 3.81 \times 10^{-7} \text{ rad/s}$$

To force the hypervelocity packet stream (velocity $u \approx 15\text{ km/s}$) to track the misaligned plane, a continuous transverse acceleration $a_c$ must be applied:
$$a_c = u \cdot \dot{\alpha} \approx 15,000 \text{ m/s} \times 3.81 \times 10^{-7} \text{ rad/s} \approx 5.71 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m/s}^2$$

Over a one-month operation window ($\Delta t \approx 30\text{ days} = 2.592 \times 10^6\text{ s}$), this acceleration translates to a cumulative steering velocity change:
$$\Delta v_{\text{steer}} = a_c \Delta t \approx 14.8 \text{ km/s/month}$$

By utilizing the precession-matched geometry derived in Section 2.3, the relative plane drift is identically zero ($\dot{\alpha} = 0$), dropping the active steering $\Delta v$ from **$14.8\text{ km/s/month}$ to zero**.

## 3. Electrodynamic Tether (EDT) Attitude Control

### 3.1 Geomagnetic Dipole Field Model & Lorentz Force
The Earth's geomagnetic field is modeled as a tilted magnetic dipole:
$$\mathbf{B}(\mathbf{r}) = \frac{\mu_0 M_E}{4\pi r^3} \left[ 3(\hat{\mathbf{m}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{r}})\hat{\mathbf{r}} - \hat{\mathbf{m}} \right]$$

where:
- $\mu_0 = 4\pi \times 10^{-7} \text{ H/m}$ (vacuum permeability)
- $M_E \approx 7.94 \times 10^{22} \text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^2$ (Earth's magnetic dipole moment)
- $\hat{\mathbf{m}}$ is the unit vector along the magnetic dipole axis
- $\mathbf{r}$ is the position vector of the shepherding node from the Earth's center, with unit vector $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$.

Let the shepherding node deploy an electrodynamic tether array of length $L$ along the unit vector $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_t$. When a control current $I(t)$ is driven through the tether, the integrated Lorentz force is:
$$\mathbf{F}_L = I(t) L (\hat{\mathbf{u}}_t \times \mathbf{B})$$

The torque vector $\mathbf{T}_{\text{EDT}}$ exerted about the shepherding node's center of mass is:
$$\mathbf{T}_{\text{EDT}} = \frac{1}{2} I(t) L^2 (\hat{\mathbf{u}}_t \times (\hat{\mathbf{u}}_t \times \mathbf{B}))$$

### 3.2 3D Rigid-Body Rotational Dynamics
The rotational state of the shepherding node is governed by Euler's equations of motion:
$$\mathbf{I}_{\text{station}} \dot{\boldsymbol{\omega}} + \boldsymbol{\omega} \times (\mathbf{I}_{\text{station}} \boldsymbol{\omega}) = \mathbf{T}_{\text{gg}} + \mathbf{T}_{\text{EDT}} + \mathbf{T}_{\text{packet}} + \mathbf{T}_{\text{pert}}$$

where:
- $\mathbf{I}_{\text{station}} = \operatorname{diag}(I_{xx}, I_{yy}, I_{zz})$ is the station's moment of inertia tensor.
- $\boldsymbol{\omega} = [\omega_x, \omega_y, \omega_z]^T$ is the angular velocity vector of the station in the body-fixed frame.
- $\mathbf{T}_{\text{gg}}$ is the gravity-gradient torque vector:
  $$\mathbf{T}_{\text{gg}} = \frac{3 \mu_E}{r^3} \hat{\mathbf{r}} \times (\mathbf{I}_{\text{station}} \hat{\mathbf{r}})$$
- $\mathbf{T}_{\text{packet}}$ is the torque induced by packet deflection forces:
  $$\mathbf{T}_{\text{packet}} = \mathbf{r}_{\text{stator}} \times \mathbf{F}_{\text{deflect}}$$
  where $\mathbf{r}_{\text{stator}}$ is the position vector of the deflection channel stator relative to the node's center of mass, and $\mathbf{F}_{\text{deflect}}$ is the packet deflection force vector.

### 3.3 Closed-Loop Feedback Controller
To actively stabilize the attitude perturbations and offset the torque spikes from packet transit, we define a proportional-derivative (PD) controller that modulates the tether current $I(t)$:
$$I(t) = -K_p e_\theta - K_d \dot{e}_\theta$$

where:
- $e_\theta = \theta(t) - \theta_{\text{ref}}$ is the attitude angle tracking error.
- $\dot{e}_\theta = \omega(t) - \omega_{\text{ref}}$ is the angular velocity error.
- $K_p$ and $K_d$ are positive feedback gains.

Because the direction of the control torque $\mathbf{T}_{\text{EDT}}$ is constrained to be orthogonal to both the tether direction $\hat{\mathbf{u}}_t$ and the local geomagnetic field $\mathbf{B}$, we utilize an orthogonal triad of tethers ($L_x, L_y, L_z$) along the principal axes. The net torque vector is:
$$\mathbf{T}_{\text{EDT, net}} = \frac{1}{2} \sum_{k \in \{x,y,z\}} I_k(t) L_k^2 (\hat{\mathbf{e}}_k \times (\hat{\mathbf{e}}_k \times \mathbf{B}))$$

Evaluating this system demonstrates that full three-axis attitude control authority is maintained at all orbital positions, except when the local field $\mathbf{B}$ aligns perfectly with one of the tether axes. This singularity is avoided via high-frequency attitude dither control.

### 3.4 Propellantless Station Keeping & Energy Harvesting
A key capability of the EDT system is its dual-mode operation. When the station's attitude is stable, the tether is operated in **generator mode**. The orbital motion of the tether through the geomagnetic field induces a motional Electromotive Force (EMF) $\mathcal{E}$:
$$\mathcal{E} = (\mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}} \times \mathbf{B}) \cdot \mathbf{L}$$

where $\mathbf{v}_{\text{rel}} = \mathbf{v}_{\text{orbit}} - \mathbf{v}_{\text{atmosphere}} \approx 4.93\text{ km/s}$ for a circular orbit at $a = 16,378\text{ km}$.

The electrical power harvested by drawing a current $I_{\text{harvest}}$ through the plasma-tether circuit is:
$$P_{\text{harvest}} = \mathcal{E} I_{\text{harvest}} - I_{\text{harvest}}^2 R_{\text{tether}}$$

For a $10\text{ km}$ tether with a linear resistance $R_{\text{tether}} \approx 100 \ \Omega$ operating in MEO:
- Local field strength $B \approx 3 \times 10^{-6}\text{ T}$
- Motional EMF $\mathcal{E} \approx v_{\text{orbit}} B L \approx 4930\text{ m/s} \times (3 \times 10^{-6}\text{ T}) \times 10000\text{ m} \approx 147.9\text{ V}$
- Maximum harvested power (impedance matched):
  $$P_{\text{max}} = \frac{\mathcal{E}^2}{4 R_{\text{tether}}} \approx \frac{(147.9)^2}{400} \approx 54.7\text{ W}$$

This continuous harvested power is stored in supercapacitor banks on board the node. When a hypervelocity packet transits the deflector, the stored energy is pulsed into the active trim coils or the EDT system at high current levels ($I \ge 100\text{ A}$), delivering megawatts of peak control torque with **zero propellant consumption**.

## 4. Packet Drift & Libration Capture Window

### 4.1 Cumulative Non-Deterministic Ballistic Drift
During the $760,000\text{ km}$ round-trip cislunar transit, free-flying packets are subject to multiple environmental perturbations. The nominal trajectory is targeted precisely using high-fidelity gravitational models, but non-deterministic perturbations introduce positional drift.

The primary non-deterministic perturbations are:
1. **Solar Radiation Pressure (SRP) Fluctuations**:
   $$a_{\text{SRP}} = \frac{\Phi_{\text{Sun}} C_R A}{c m_p} \approx 7.78 \times 10^{-9} \text{ m/s}^2$$
   where $\Phi_{\text{Sun}} \approx 1361 \text{ W/m}^2$, packet mass $m_p = 35 \text{ kg}$, cross-sectional area $A \approx 0.05 \text{ m}^2$, and reflectivity uncertainty $\delta C_R \approx 0.1$.
2. **Third-Body Solar Tidal Fluctuations**:
   $$a_{\text{Sun, tidal}} \approx \frac{2 G M_S D}{r_{ES}^3} \approx 3.0 \times 10^{-5} \text{ m/s}^2$$
3. **Launch Execution Errors**:
   $$\delta v_0 \approx 1.0 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m/s} \quad (1.0\text{ mm/s}), \quad \delta r_0 \approx 1.0 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m} \quad (1.0\text{ mm})$$

The transit time for a one-way distance $D = 380,000\text{ km}$ at a velocity $u = 15\text{ km/s}$ is:
$$t_{\text{transit}} = \frac{380,000\text{ km}}{15\text{ km/s}} \approx 25,333\text{ s} \approx 7.04\text{ hours}$$

The cumulative transverse drift $\Delta x_{\text{drift}}$ at the receiving node is modeled as:
$$\Delta x_{\text{drift}} \approx \sqrt{ \left( \delta v_0 t_{\text{transit}} \right)^2 + \left( \frac{1}{2} \delta a_{\text{pert}} t_{\text{transit}}^2 \right)^2 }$$

where $\delta a_{\text{pert}} \approx 1.0 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m/s}^2$ represents the combined non-deterministic perturbation uncertainty (solar wind variation and gravity model truncation). Evaluating this yield:
$$\Delta x_{\text{launch}} = 1.0 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m/s} \times 25,333 \text{ s} \approx 25.33\text{ m}$$
$$\Delta x_{\text{pert}} = \frac{1}{2} (1.0 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m/s}^2) (25,333 \text{ s})^2 \approx 32.09\text{ m}$$
$$\Delta x_{\text{drift, one-way}} = \sqrt{25.33^2 + 32.09^2} \approx 40.88\text{ m}$$

For the full round-trip corridor ($2D = 760,000\text{ km}$, $t_{\text{transit}} \approx 50,667\text{ s}$):
$$\Delta x_{\text{launch}} = 1.0 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m/s} \times 50,667 \text{ s} \approx 50.67\text{ m}$$
$$\Delta x_{\text{pert}} = \frac{1}{2} (1.0 \times 10^{-7} \text{ m/s}^2) (50,667 \text{ s})^2 \approx 128.36\text{ m}$$
$$\Delta x_{\text{drift, round-trip}} = \sqrt{50.67^2 + 128.36^2} \approx 138.01\text{ m}$$

This confirms that the cumulative ballistic drift over the cislunar transit lies in the range of **$40 - 140\text{ m}$**.

### 4.2 Hybrid Capture Window Mechanics
To achieve $100\%$ packet capture efficiency without physical guide-tubes, we implement a **Hybrid Capture Window** at the receiving nodes.

```
                           HYBRID CAPTURE WINDOW ENVELOPE
    
     <--------------------------- 150 meters --------------------------->
    [ Active Tracking Zone ] ----> [ Active Trim Coils ] ----> [ Passive Halbach ]
                                                                 (Aperture: 2m)
```

1. **Passive Centering (SmCo Halbach Array)**:
   - Centering Stiffness: $k_{\text{passive}} \approx 3,000\text{ N/m}$
   - Aperture Radius: $R_{\text{aperture}} \approx 2.0\text{ m}$
   - If a packet arrives within the $2.0\text{ m}$ aperture, the passive magnetic centering force is sufficient to center and lock the packet into the deflection stator channel.
2. **Active Steering Array (Pulsed Trim Coils)**:
   - Upstream Tracking: A lidar/RF tracking array located $d_{\text{track}} \approx 5,000\text{ km}$ upstream of the node detects the packet's drift. The available correction time is:
     $$t_{\text{capture}} = \frac{5,000\text{ km}}{15\text{ km/s}} \approx 333\text{ s}$$
   - The active trim coils generate a pulsed magnetic field gradient that interacts with the packet's magnetic core dipole moment ($\mathbf{m}_p \approx 500 \text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^2$):
     $$\mathbf{F}_{\text{active}} = \nabla (\mathbf{m}_p \cdot \mathbf{B}_{\text{active}})$$
   - To correct a maximum drift $\Delta x \approx 150\text{ m}$ over the capture time $t_{\text{capture}} \approx 333\text{ s}$, the required correction acceleration is:
     $$a_{\text{correct}} = \frac{2 \Delta x}{t_{\text{capture}}^2} = \frac{300}{(333)^2} \approx 2.70 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m/s}^2$$
   - The corresponding transverse force on the $35\text{ kg}$ packet is:
     $$F_{\text{active}} = m_p a_{\text{correct}} = 35\text{ kg} \times 2.70 \times 10^{-3} \text{ m/s}^2 \approx 0.0945\text{ N}$$
   - Resolving this force for the magnetic gradient yields:
     $$\frac{\partial B}{\partial x} = \frac{F_{\text{active}}}{m_p} = \frac{0.0945\text{ N}}{500 \text{ A}\cdot\text{m}^2} \approx 1.89 \times 10^{-4} \text{ T/m}$$

This magnetic gradient is extremely small and can be easily generated by lightweight electromagnetic coils. The active capture window successfully guides packets with up to **$150\text{ m}$ of ballistic drift** back into the $2\text{ m}$ passive centering core, verifying the physical viability of open-air packet shepherding.
